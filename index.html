Research

My research uses theory and experiment together to make catalytic processes cleaner and more efficient. Three active themes are shown below, followed by my research vision and experience.

Emission Control

Passive NOₓ adsorption on Pd-CHA zeolites: a complete mechanism for NOₓ uptake and oxidation on dynamically hydrated Pd sites, where water and CO shape the active site.

  • DFT benchmarking and vibrational analysis tied to IR fingerprints
  • Bench-scale flow reactor experiments on monolith Pd/zeolite

Dynamic Catalysis

Rich–lean feed cycling for methane partial oxidation to syngas on Pt/Al₂O₃, and surface charge as a lever on CO binding on Pt in a catalytic condenser.

  • Periodic operation to reach non-steady-state surface coverages
  • DFT charge distribution and CO binding energy versus surface charge

CO₂ Electrochemical Conversion

Cerium-based electrocatalysts that turn CO₂ into hard carbon, where oxygen vacancies and the Ce³⁺/Ce⁴⁺ redox couple control nucleation and C–C coupling.

  • DFT mechanism under electrochemical conditions
  • Link between computed active sites and scalable catalyst design

Research vision

I aim to discover materials through descriptor-based screening and data science, and to validate the predictions with experiments, so that theory and real-world applications such as methane to syngas and CO₂ valorization stay connected.

1. Materials discoveryDFT, machine learning and kinetic modeling to screen catalysts with descriptors
2. Mechanistic investigationDFT and ab initio molecular dynamics for reaction pathways and active sites
3. Experimental validationLab-scale reactions and in situ characterization such as DRIFTS

Dynamic catalysis

Vary feed composition and cycle times to reach coverages that steady state cannot, and use DRIFTS together with DFT to identify key intermediates.

CO₂ utilization

Metals, binary and ternary alloys for CO₂ hydrogenation, studied with DFT and machine learning, with descriptor-based screening.

Depolymerization and zeolite water

Reactivity of water inside zeolite pores, a key factor for depolymerization and other liquid-phase reactions.

Teaching Interests

Alongside a rigorous research program, I am deeply committed to undergraduate and graduate education in chemical engineering. My teaching rests on three principles.

Student-Centered Learning

Fostering instructor–student rapport, involving students in course design, and using cooperative learning to build collaborative skills.

Bridging Theory and Practice

Case studies, hands-on projects and industry-relevant examples that connect fundamentals with real-world application.

Continuous Improvement

Seeking student feedback through surveys, office hours and post-exam reflections to adapt teaching to evolving needs.

Courses

Undergraduate: I am prepared to teach chemical reaction engineering, mass transfer and heat transfer.

Graduate: I aspire to develop an advanced course in reaction engineering and to design a course on computational methods in catalysis that integrates DFT, kinetic modeling and data-driven workflows.

Experience: Teaching assistant for Analytical Methods for Chemical Engineers (CHEE 3321) and Bio & Physical Chemistry (CHEE 3466) at the University of Houston, which strengthened my commitment to transformative, inclusive instruction.
